Executive Interview
Vineet Trakroo
CEO & Chief Evolution Officer, Evolution Strategy Advisors LLP
1. You often say growth problems are rarely just sales or marketing problems. What is usually
hiding underneath?
Sales is merely the effect, the final leg of the entire commercial engine. If sales figures fall short of targets,
diagnosing the problem requires rigorous inquiry into the underlying root causes. Because multiple functions
contribute to performance, the bottlenecks rarely sit in a single department.
Underneath sluggish numbers, you typically find deeper, softer organizational friction: misaligned culture, leadership
gaps, flawed strategic choices, or team competency deficits. Even delivery processes and poor customer
experience design can quietly derail top-line momentum.
Fortunately, modern organizations generate rich departmental data, making diagnostic discovery much faster:
Operational Friction: Data reveals whether the drag comes from supply chain inefficiencies, customer satisfaction
drop-offs, or poor sales productivity. The real story is always in the data; leadership just needs to look deeper.
Outdated Metrics: Many legacy, brick-and-mortar companies still evaluate teams using Key Performance
Indicators (KPIs) designed for how business was conducted two to three decades ago. Today, many enterprises
generate 30% to 50% of their business from digital channels and digital is where the incremental growth resides.
Departmental performance metrics must be comprehensively revamped to reflect this modern commercial reality.
2. When a large B2C business starts losing growth momentum, what is the first thing you look
for?
Over the past decade, several transformative forces have begun impacting enterprise growth. The first diagnostic
question is foundational: What are we selling, to whom, and has it become a commodity? In most cases, product
differentiation has eroded. Consumers today are highly informed; they scrutinize ingredient decks and verify efficacy
claims. Even category-defining brands have lost significant market share because of this shift. Entire categories
such as fairness creams have faced massive market corrections driven by evolving consumer and social
awareness.
Beyond the product itself, seismic shifts in the operating environment must be evaluated:
Technological Obsolescence: Rapid tech advancements (even before factoring in the full impact of AI) can render
existing value propositions obsolete overnight.
Geopolitical & Supply Chain Volatility: Shifting trade barriers can close core markets and force organizations
back to the drawing board.
Changing Demographic Behaviours: Generations X, Y, Z, and Alpha consume, evaluate, and purchase through
entirely different journeys, resetting expectations around distribution speed and convenience.
Demand-Generation Disruption: Traditional B2C drivers advertising, media planning, and creative messaging are
navigating their own existential crossroads.
The real question for leadership is organizational agility: Has your business adapted to these disruptions, or are you
executing a decade-old playbook? All traditional 4Ps of marketing are being redefined. Yesterday’s competitive
moats whether in product formulation, exclusive distribution, heavy ad spending, or pricing power are under active
challenge.
This explains why established brands are increasingly replacing CEOs: boards recognize the urgent need to align
operating models with how younger demographics buy. Furthermore, while Digital-First (D2C) brands excel online,

3. How do you identify the difference between a growth problem and an execution problem?
A Growth Problem is Strategic: It is tied to market headroom, category choices, and positioning. It can often be
solved through tactical pivots with fast turnarounds, followed by robust Go-To-Market (GTM) strategies that build
enduring value. For example, if a brand hits a 50% market share in a mature segment, headroom naturally
diminishes; finding new growth requires targeting entirely new customer segments.
An Execution Problem is Systemic: It represents a structural leak in the bucket. If sales productivity is chronically
low, the issue is operational. Fixing execution takes significantly longer because it requires re-engineering
infrastructure, implementing new standard operating procedures, and driving organizational adoption.
While distinct, growth and execution are interdependent. A brilliant strategic growth thesis will fail if the execution
engine cannot deliver.
4. You have worked across FMCG, automobiles, pharma, consumer products, and building
materials. What growth lesson works across these very different industries?
The most universal commercial bottlenecks are rooted in execution breakdowns. These typically stem from poor
strategic guidance, lack of clear prioritization, capability gaps, and the absence of crisp operating frameworks.
The Communication Disconnect: Internal communication is often surprisingly weak. Frontline sales teams rarely
have absolute clarity on priorities, so they simply do their best with what they know. Crucially, on-the-ground market
friction rarely makes it up to executive leadership unfiltered, leading to boardroom decisions made on outdated
assumptions.
Timely In-Market Diagnostics: Practical execution friction is inevitable. The differentiator between winning and
losing companies is how frequently leadership audits ground realities and implements timely corrections. Planning
must be anchored in ground-level market realities, sales force competencies, and channel partner incentives.
The Trade Intermediary Priority: While consumer-centricity is vital, distribution-led B2C businesses must
remember that the distributor, stockist, dealer, or retailer is the first customer to satisfy. If channel partners are not
aligned, incentivized, and supported, your product will never reach the consumer effectively.
5. You emphasize doing less with more intensity. How can CEOs decide what to stop?
Deciding what not to do is the single most critical decision a CEO will ever make. It requires ruthless focus. An
organization cannot pursue multiple high-stakes objectives simultaneously without diluting execution quality.
Impact-Led Prioritization: Initiatives must be prioritized based on what drives maximum impact on core bottom-line
outcomes. This priority stack should be recalibrated quarterly as capabilities mature and bottlenecks are resolved.
Unified Organizational Alignment: Every department must rally around a single, shared operational agenda to
eliminate siloed cross-purposes.
Data-Driven Gap Analysis: Advanced analytics should be leveraged to surface frontline sales friction, channel
margin pain points, and strategic execution gaps.
Competency and Culture Mapping: Benchmark internal capabilities against industry leaders. However,
sophisticated frameworks and analytics are useless without a high-performance culture committed to accountability
and delivery.
6. How can businesses turn consumer insights into actual growth rather than just research
reports?
Converting research into commercial growth requires an institutionalized, repeatable process.

Tracking Usage & Attitude (U&A): As consumers use a product or service over time, their expectations evolve
from primary functional needs to secondary conveniences such as packaging functionality, dosage precision, and
lifestyle alignment. Continuous U&A tracking is essential to fine-tune marketing mix inputs, product design, and
delivery formats.
Bridging Insights to NPD: Market research must not sit inside the marketing department as an academic deck.
Leading enterprises maintain structured New Product Development (NPD) and innovation pipelines that translate
customer feedback into packaging changes, SKU variants, or entirely new offerings.
Executing the Adaptation: Where internal teams struggle to translate research data into commercialized offerings,
leveraging external advisory frameworks can accelerate pipeline execution and market launch.
7. In your experience, why do strong strategies often fail during execution?
In terms of organizational effort, strategy is 10%; execution is 90%. Even a sound strategy fails when execution is
neglected:
The "Business as Usual" Trap: Teams default to comfortable routines. Actions become unthinking muscle
memory rather than active responses to a dynamic market.
Organizational Blind Spots: Teams operating too close to daily operations frequently miss emerging structural
problems.
The "Iceberg Effect" (Frontline Distortion): As management theorist Sidney Yoshida pointed out, less than 10%
of frontline operational problems ever reach top management. Feedback from dealers and frontline sales teams
gets diluted like a game of telephone on its way to headquarters, causing leadership to operate in a vacuum.
Data Without Actionable Analytics: Smaller companies often under-invest in understanding why products are
chosen or rejected. Conversely, large enterprises drown in digitized sales data but lack the analytical rigor to identify
where customers drop off in their purchase journey.
8. What separates a business that is growing today from one that is genuinely future-ready?
Current revenue growth does not equal future-readiness. Current top-line growth can easily be an illusion sustained
by historical distribution moats, broad economic tailwinds, or weak competitors.
The Agility Gap: Consider legacy giants with near-monopolistic distribution and marketing muscle competing
against new-age players. A digital-native brand like Mamaearth achieved scale rapidly, outpacing long-standing
legacy brands in specific segments despite having none of the legacy distribution infrastructure. One company is
growing on historical momentum; the other is built for the modern consumer journey.
Adapting Modern Capabilities: When a global legacy FMCG powerhouse must lean on a D2C partner to manage
digital marketing for its legacy personal care portfolio, it signals a deeper struggle to build internal, future-ready
capabilities.
The True Mark of Future-Readiness: A future-ready organization is deeply customer-centric. It systematically
maps shifts in consumer journeys and leverages modern tech and AI to deliver hyper-personalized value
propositions. This agility is the foundation of sustainable long-term compounding.
9. After working with 100+ brands, what is one recurring mistake you see established B2C
businesses making?
In business, there are no pure mistakes only learnings. That said, the single largest gap between sustained market
leadership and stagnation is failing to adapt offerings and go-to-market models as consumer buying habits evolve.
Product Assumptions vs. Consumer Validation: Tweaking product formulations, packaging, or customer
touchpoints without direct consumer validation is a recipe for value destruction.
Force-Fitting Across Segments: Designing an offering for one niche and force-fitting it across divergent consumer
segments leads to brand dilution and weak sales.

Diluting Strategic Capacity: Organizations frequently try to solve too many initiatives at once, creating competing
internal priorities. Real transformation requires matching strategic ambitions with internal capability constraints,
executing systematically phase by phase.
10. Looking toward 2030, what should Indian B2C leaders start changing today to build
sustainable growth?
Building sustainable growth by 2030 requires one core capability: the institutional agility to continuously sense and
respond to internal and external environmental changes.
Relentless Focus: Sustainable success does not come from doing everything; it comes from identifying the few
commercial drivers that truly move the needle and executing them with extreme rigor.
Active Environmental Scanning: Leadership teams must proactively monitor market shifts, technological
disruptions, and evolving customer journeys, willing to reshape operating models to drive customer delight.
Customer Obsession as the North Star: Restructuring corporate hierarchies, modifying culture, or adopting
advanced digital platforms are enablers. In an era where product features commoditize rapidly, the ultimate source
of enterprise value remains the customer’s end-to-end journey, usage experience, and overall satisfaction.
